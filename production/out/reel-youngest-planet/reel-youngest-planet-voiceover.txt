Earth is four and a half billion years old. This planet is not even one million. It is called Elias 2-24 b, and it is the youngest planet ever found. It beats the previous record holder by a wide margin. It weighs in at about two Jupiter masses, sits about four hundred and fifty light-years away, and it is still embedded in the disk of material it formed from. Nobody pointed a new telescope at it. The team, led by Andrea Bernardi at Universidad Diego Portales, found it in archival data from the Keck Observatory. The paper is in the Astrophysical Journal Letters. Source in the caption.
