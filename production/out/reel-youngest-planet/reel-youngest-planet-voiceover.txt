Earth is four and a half billion years old. This planet is not even one million. It is called Elias 2-24 b, and it is the youngest planet ever found. The previous record holders are all over five million years old. It is roughly Jupiter's mass, though the models give anything from about two to four Jupiters. It sits about four hundred and fifty light-years away, still embedded in the disk of material it formed from, about fifty five astronomical units out from its star. Nobody pointed a new telescope at it. The team, led by Andrea Bernardi at Universidad Diego Portales, found it in archival data from the Keck Observatory. The paper is in the Astrophysical Journal Letters. Source in the caption.
