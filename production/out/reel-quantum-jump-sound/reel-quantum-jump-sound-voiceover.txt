Sound does not fade away smoothly. At the smallest scale, it jumps. Sound is vibration, and vibration is energy. At the quantum level that energy comes in packets called phonons. A phonon can only sit on fixed energy steps, never in between. Physicists at Stanford, in the Safavi-Naeini lab, built a tiny mechanical resonator and watched single phonons jump between those steps in real time. It is the first real-time observation of a quantum jump in sound. The result is in the journal Science this month. One note.
