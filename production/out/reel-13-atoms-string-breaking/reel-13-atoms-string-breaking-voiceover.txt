Thirteen atoms just simulated one of the strangest ideas in particle physics. Pull confined particles apart hard enough, and instead of separating, they snap into new pairs. Physicists call it string breaking. Quarks are the classic example, and that is an analogy here, not the experiment. A team at the Duke Quantum Center held thirteen ytterbium ions in a line and used them to simulate the string. When it broke, the new pairs formed at the ends first and spread inward, rather than appearing evenly along the string. That edge-driven pattern is the result. To be clear, this is a quantum simulation of a lattice model. No real quarks, no real vacuum. De and Monroe, Nature Physics, September twenty twenty six. Source in the caption.
