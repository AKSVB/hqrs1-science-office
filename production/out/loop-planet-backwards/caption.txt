How does a planet end up orbiting backwards? (No one knows yet: no known companion explains it.)
Astronomy & Astrophysics, Sept 2026; arXiv 2609.24870. Carteret, University of Geneva; NIRPS, La Silla.

GJ 3090 b, 73 light-years away, orbits its red dwarf tilted about 136 degrees to the star's spin: past sideways, into backwards. It is 2.2 times Earth's radius, 4.5 times its mass, and its year lasts 2.9 days. It is the first retrograde planet found around a red dwarf. Every planet in our solar system goes round the way the Sun spins, so something knocked this one over, and no massive companion has been found to explain it. Images are generated illustrations.
