Something is reproducing at one hundred and forty five degrees Fahrenheit. And it is not a bacterium. It is an amoeba, a complex cell like the ones in your body, found in a hot stream in Lassen Volcanic National Park. Meet Incendiamoeba cascadensis, the fire amoeba. It divides at one hundred and forty five degrees, and it survived a five-minute spike to one hundred and fifty eight in a protective state. The old limit for complex life was about one hundred and forty. The record moved by only a few degrees, but it moved. The study, from Syracuse University, is in the journal Cell this month.
