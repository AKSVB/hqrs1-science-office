You do not have one brain. You have two. That is the headline from Stanford this month. In the embryo, two separate populations of stem cells build the brain. One group, marked by a gene called Otx2, builds the forebrain and midbrain. The other, marked by Gbx2, builds the hindbrain. They never mix, and they carry different chromatin states from the earliest stages. The same split shows up in chicks, zebrafish and even acorn worms. That suggests two nervous systems that evolved separately and fused hundreds of millions of years ago. One caveat. The paper describes two cell lineages. Two organs is the press release talking.
