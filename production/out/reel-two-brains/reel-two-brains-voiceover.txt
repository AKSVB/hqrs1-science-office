Your brain is built by two cell lineages that never mix. That is the finding from Stanford this month. In the mouse embryo, two separate populations of stem cells build the brain, and they are separate from gastrulation onward. One group, marked by a gene called Otx2, builds the forebrain and midbrain. The other, marked by Gbx2, builds the hindbrain. They carry different chromatin states from the earliest stages. The same split shows up in chicks, zebrafish and acorn worms, lineages that diverged around five hundred and fifty million years ago. One caveat.
