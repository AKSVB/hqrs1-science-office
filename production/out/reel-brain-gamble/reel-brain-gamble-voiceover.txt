A brain signal can predict your gamble about half a second before the choice is made. That is the finding from a study in Nature Neuroscience, published on the fifteenth of September. Six patients, already fitted with electrodes for surgical monitoring, played a video game with hallways, bombs and treasure chests. Take the risk, or avoid it. In the orbitofrontal cortex, two patches about two centimetres apart did opposite jobs. One became active before a risky choice, the other before a safe one. Together, they predicted the decision roughly half a second before it was made. Six patients, one video game, and no claim about free will. Starkweather, Chang and Knight, at UCSF and UC Berkeley.
