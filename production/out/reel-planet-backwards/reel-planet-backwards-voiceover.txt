There is a planet orbiting its star backwards, and tilted. Every planet in our solar system goes round in the same direction the Sun spins. This one does not. G J three zero nine zero b sits seventy three light-years away, around a red dwarf star. It is about two point two times Earth's radius, four and a half times its mass, and its year lasts two point nine days. Its orbit is tilted about one hundred and thirty six degrees: past sideways, into backwards. It is the first retrograde planet found around a red dwarf, and no known companion explains how it got there. Yann Carteret and colleagues, University of Geneva, with the NIRPS spectrograph in Chile. Astronomy and Astrophysics, September twenty twenty six. Source in the caption.
