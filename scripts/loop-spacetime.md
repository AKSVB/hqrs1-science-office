## 3. Voiced variant, 40 s (104 words, deep calm delivery, bed A under, karaoke band on)

The Sun does not pull the planets. It warps space-time. School drew gravity as a force, a tug across empty space. Einstein's picture is different. Mass curves space-time, and anything moving through it follows the straightest path it can. For Earth, near the Sun, that path is an orbit. One caveat: this dented sheet is a two-dimensional stand-in for curvature in four dimensions, three of space and one of time. The proof came in 1919. During a total eclipse, starlight grazing the Sun bent by about 1.75 arcseconds, as Einstein predicted. So why does Earth keep circling? That is the straightest road there is.

Band numbers as digits: "1919", "1.75 arcseconds". Hook line for the voiced cut: line 1 above. The voiced cut may use plate L for the 1919 sentence and plate F for "School drew gravity as a force".

## 4. Caption (loop card and voiced cut)

If nothing pulls Earth, why does it not fly off? (It coasts on the straightest path through space-time the Sun has curved, and that path closes into an orbit.)
Einstein, Die Feldgleichungen der Gravitation, Prussian Academy, 25 Nov 1915. Dyson, Eddington and Davidson, 1919 eclipse results, Royal Society, 6 Nov 1919.

Newton's textbook picture is a force tugging across empty space. Einstein's is geometry: mass curves space-time and free objects follow its straightest lines. Einstein predicted starlight grazing the Sun bends by 1.75 arcseconds, twice Newton's value. Two expeditions measured it at the 29 May 1919 eclipse and got his number. The dented grid is a 2D analogy of 4D curvature. Images are generated illustrations.
