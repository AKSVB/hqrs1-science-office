## 2. Shot list and grammar per archetype

Cross-archetype facts the shot lists rely on:
- Roughly half of viewers leave in the first three seconds; reels holding above 60% at three seconds outperform those under 40% by 5 to 10x in reach (https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-reels-3-second-hook-leads). Since August 2025 Instagram reports a skip rate (share leaving inside 3 s) and a retention curve; Mosseri names watch time, sends per reach and likes per reach as the main ranking signals (https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/).
- Captioned reels complete at 65% vs 37% uncaptioned in Zebracat's 2025 dataset (https://www.zebracat.ai/post/instagram-reels-statistics); word-by-word captions give a new fixation point every 250 to 400 ms, which is why they hold the 1.5 to 3.5 s cliff (https://emax.studio/blog/word-by-word-ai-captions-vs-static-subtitles). Karaoke captions lift completion 15 to 30% over static subtitles on talking-head content (https://ascynd.io/en/blog/why-hormozi-captions-get-more-views).
- Cut cadence: TikTok tolerates cuts every 1.5 to 3 s, Reels 2.5 to 4 s, Shorts 3 to 5 s for tutorial content; static shots over 5 s hurt retention (https://cutscore.io/blog/how-fast-should-i-cut-my-video, https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-pacing-editing-guide).
- Length: 7 to 15 s reels complete at 74% on average vs 49% for 30 to 60 s (https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-instagram-reels-length); TikTok's "viral sweet spot" is 24 to 38 s (https://nealschaffer.com/tiktok-video-length/).

### (A) 3D/animated explainer (Zack D Films, Kurzgesagt, quantumfracture, AsapSCIENCE, Kok Bisa)
- 0-1 s: a rendered close-up of an ordinary object or body part already mid-action (needle entering skin, a splinter under skin, a McFlurry spoon), with the narrator's first clause already playing. Zack's three most viewed shorts are exactly this: "How Stitches Work" (214M), "How Splinters Get Unstuck" (180M), "Why the McFlurry Spoon Looks Weird" (143M) (https://zackdfilms.fandom.com/wiki/Zack_D._Films). The script format is "hook in the first 1 to 2 seconds, then a fast explanation", 65 to 95 words for roughly 30 s (https://github.com/Anil-matcha/zack-d-films-ai-video-generator/blob/main/SKILL.md, https://www.snaplama.com/blog/how-to-create-animation-videos-like-zack-d-films-in-2026).
- 1-3 s: camera pushes in or orbits the object; the question is stated ("Have you ever wondered...") which became so recognisable it is parodied (https://celebtechbuzz.com/zack-d-films/).
- 3-15 s: 4 to 6 scenes, each a clean, sparsely lit 3D set with one character or one mechanism; cross-sections and X-ray reveals are the workhorse transition (https://www.snaplama.com/blog/how-to-create-animation-videos-like-zack-d-films-in-2026). Kurzgesagt equivalents: flat vector scenes, ~200 unique illustrations per 10 minutes, visual metaphors storyboarded before animation (https://10.studio/the-incredible-amount-of-work-behind-kurzgesagts-beautiful-animated-videos/).
- Second hook (~10-15 s): the "unsettling twist" beat, where the mechanism goes wrong or the scale changes (the format "peels back the layers of everyday reality, often with a slightly unsettling twist", https://celebtechbuzz.com/zack-d-films/).
- Ending: payoff line, then hard stop; no outro. Loops are common because the ending frame resembles the opening frame.
- Captions: animated captions synced to narration, punchy short lines rather than full sentences (https://celebtechbuzz.com/zack-d-films/); Kurzgesagt uses little on-screen text and relies on the image.
- Cuts: every 2 to 4 s, but camera moves inside a shot substitute for cuts (slow orbit, push-in).
- Numbers on screen: rare in Zack D Films; Kurzgesagt uses big comparative scale graphics.
- Sound: calm, processed, general-American narration mistaken for AI (https://celebtechbuzz.com/zack-d-films/); a single recognisable background track ("the signature music is the background track that defines the format's atmosphere", https://taletok.io/zack-d-films-ai-generator/); Zack's production stack is Blender, Cascadeur, EmberGen, After Effects with templated compositing and a Flamenco render farm (https://www.chrisreillyportfolio.com/zackdfilms.php).
- CTA: none in-video; the loop and the next short are the CTA.
- Colour and type: high-contrast neon palette for Kurzgesagt (pinks, blues, oranges on dark), rounded shapes (https://midlibrary.io/styles/kurzgesagt, https://artsatmichigan.umich.edu/ink/2019/09/27/simple-bright-beautiful-the-work-of-kurzgesagt); Zack uses muted photoreal sets with one lit subject.

### (B) Real demo / lab / physical experiment (physicsfun, Action Lab, NileRed, Steve Mould, Mark Rober, Mamix, Manual do Mundo)
- 0-1 s: the object already moving in a locked-off, tightly framed shot; no title card. physicsfun videos are 6 to 30 s "carefully planned and choreographed instructional demonstrations" whose result "appears reality-defying" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_E._Hall).
- 1-3 s: the unexpected behaviour happens (a top that shouldn't balance, a liquid that shouldn't change colour). NileRed treats lab work "as both rigorous science and visual spectacle" (https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/nilered/).
- 3-15 s: one or two angles only; a slow re-run or a macro insert. Narration names the principle in one sentence.
- Second hook: the reveal of what it is or a scale-up (bigger version, different material). Mark Rober's 2025 Tesla foam-wall test is the long-form version: setup, then the crash, 20M views in two days (https://www.popsci.com/technology/mark-rober-tesla-foam-wall-video/).
- Ending: the demo repeats once more clean, often as a loop; caption text carries the explanation. physicsfun's on-platform explanation lives in the caption, not on screen (https://www.vice.com/en/article/distract-yourself-forever-with-physicsfun-instagrams-best-science-toy-collection/).
- Captions: sparse; one line naming the object or the effect. TheDadLab posts with no voiceover at all so that it "transcends language barriers" (https://www.netinfluencer.com/sergei-urban-of-thedadlab/).
- Cuts: slow by platform standards (a 6 to 15 s single take is normal); motion inside the frame does the work.
- Numbers: temperatures, RPM, voltages appear as small corner labels when relevant.
- Sound: room sound and the object's own noise are the hook; music optional and low.
- CTA: implicit; comments are driven by "how" questions.

### (C) Talking head over b-roll (Cleo Abram, Hank Green, astro.alexandra, Kyle Hill, science.sam, Astrokobi, Santaolalla, Dr Karan Rajan)
- 0-1 s: the presenter's face, already mid-sentence, with the first clause being a news claim. Cleo Abram's template: "NASA just regained contact with humanity's most distant spacecraft" (https://async.com/blog/why-cleo-abram-goes-viral/, video at https://www.tiktok.com/@cleoabram/video/7372903747728461098). Astro Alexandra uses green-screen news reactions, "quick cuts to keep viewers engaged from the first second" (https://www.favikon.com/blog/who-is-astro-alexandra; example https://www.tiktok.com/@astro_alexandra/video/7519204203206675725).
- 1-3 s: cut to the b-roll (telescope image, archival clip, chart) with the presenter's voice continuing; Abram's shorts focus on "a single core message, a key visual, and a concise structure" (https://async.com/blog/why-cleo-abram-goes-viral/).
- 3-15 s: alternation presenter / b-roll every 2 to 3 s; bold animated text and simple graphs support each claim (https://async.com/blog/why-cleo-abram-goes-viral/). Hank Green's format is the reply video: a question from comments on screen, answered to camera (https://exploringhealth.org/2023/09/22/health-beyond-the-blog-hank-green-on-tiktok/). Dr Karan Rajan's is reaction: a circulating health clip in a picture-in-picture, then confirm or debunk (https://spotlight.ebu.ch/p/deepfake-epidemic-doctor-finds-himself).
- Second hook: "Here's why that matters" or "But here's the weird part"; Abram's structure explicitly includes "This matters because..." (https://async.com/blog/why-cleo-abram-goes-viral/).
- Ending: an optimistic one-liner and a soft follow ask ("If you like optimistic science and tech stories, subscribe for more", caption of the Voyager video above).
- Captions: large, bold, centre-lower karaoke captions on the presenter; headline-style static text on b-roll. Astro Alexandra and Karan Rajan both use large sans-serif with a black outline or box (visible in the TikTok thumbnails above; exact fonts unverified).
- Cuts: every 1.5 to 3 s, whip or jump cut between face and b-roll.
- Numbers: big single figures ("15 billion miles", "46 years old") flashed as text (Voyager script above).
- Sound: fast conversational voice, minimal music bed, occasional whoosh SFX on cuts.
- CTA: follow/subscribe; Hank Green's #askhank invites the next question (https://www.tiktok.com/discover/hank-green-tiktok?lang=en).

### (D) Institution imagery (NASA, natgeo, ESA, ESO, Webb, CERN, BBC Earth)
- 0-1 s: a single spectacular frame, no text: an eclipse, a rocket plume, a Webb field. NASA's annular eclipse highlight reel took 22.8M views (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/how-nasa-does-social-media-marketing-for-all-of-humanity-podcast/452944/ and NASA social highlights quoted there); the Carina Nebula post remains NASA's most engaged post (7M+ engagements) (https://www.enrichlabs.ai/case-study/nasa-social-media-strategy).
- 1-3 s: slow push-in or the live event unfolding; a short caption line identifies what is seen.
- 3-15 s: two or three images or clips, each held 3 to 5 s, Ken Burns on stills; light music, sometimes mission audio.
- Second hook: a crew/human moment (astronaut POV, launch-day faces). During Artemis II, NASA's flagship IG added 4.6M followers and @nasaartemis grew 2M (+66%); Sprout Social counted 100M+ TikTok and Instagram engagements in the launch week (https://www.fastcompany.com/91525305/nasa-moon-social-media-analytics); #artemis2 reached ~462.9M TikTok views (https://almcorp.com/blog/artemis-ii-social-media-stats/).
- Ending: logo or mission tag; the caption carries the science.
- Captions: minimal, small, corner or lower-third; images are the message. New Scientist's growth came from "a voice on Instagram which is both authentic to the platform and authentic to New Scientist": 99M views and 274K new followers in six months (https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/new-scientist-hits-milestone-25026).
- Cuts: slow (3 to 6 s), Ken Burns and dissolves.
- Numbers: mission stats as text overlays.
- Sound: ambient, orchestral or mission comms.
- CTA: "link in bio" to the release.

### (E) Anatomy / medical footage (Institute of Human Anatomy, Karan Rajan, Doctor Mike, VOKA)
- 0-1 s: the specimen or organ in frame with a gloved hand pointing; the question is spoken at once. IOHA's early method was literally a list of structures turned into "can I create a video around that?", then topic selection driven by comments (https://thepathologist.com/issues/2021/articles/sep/anatomy-of-a-social-media-success).
- 1-3 s: the hand moves the tissue, revealing the thing the question is about (how deep hair roots go, where the stomach actually is). The hair-depth video reached 22M TikTok views (https://www.newsweek.com/video-how-deep-hair-grows-head-cadaver-viral-tiktok-institute-human-anatomy-1641249); the pregnancy-organs video 8M (https://www.newsweek.com/tiktok-cadaver-video-pregnancy-organs-move-viewed-8-million-times-institute-human-anatomy-1635596).
- 3-15 s: a single continuous take on a fixed camera, presenter's voice off-camera; cut only for a second angle or a 3D-model insert.
- Second hook: the comparison to the viewer's own body ("this is what is happening in you right now").
- Ending: "full video on our YouTube" (every IOHA TikTok caption in the results says so, e.g. https://www.tiktok.com/@instituteofhumananatomy/video/6943237365565574406).
- Captions: short white text with dark outline, top or centre; anatomical labels as arrows.
- Cuts: slow; the hand is the camera move.
- Numbers: rare.
- Sound: presenter's voice, no music or very low.
- CTA: the YouTube pointer; Karan Rajan ends on a one-line verdict.

### (F) News/headline cards and carousels (IFLScience, New Scientist, ScienceAlert, ense_de_ciencia, quantum__physics__, Evrim Ağacı)
- 0-1 s (reel): a headline in large type over a stock or archival clip; (carousel): a cover that "feels incomplete" without swiping, a cropped diagram or a "#1 of 7" (https://carouselli.com/blog/instagram-carousel-cover-image, https://www.adpicto.com/en/blog/instagram-carousel-best-practices-2026).
- 1-3 s: the headline resolves into the first fact; Ken Burns on a still image.
- 3-15 s: three to five facts, each its own card, 2 to 4 s per card, with a source line in small type.
- Second hook: the "why it matters" card or a counterintuitive number.
- Ending: "Read more at the link in bio" or "Save this".
- Captions: full lines, headline weight (bold sans), centred; ense_de_ciencia runs 41K posts of this kind at 2M followers (https://www.instagram.com/ense_de_ciencia/).
- Cuts: 2 to 4 s per card; carousels are static.
- Numbers: the number is often the headline.
- Sound: trending audio or none.
- CTA: save/share. Carousels beat reels on engagement by 12% and on saves; reels beat carousels on reach by 36% in Buffer's 4M-post analysis (https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagram-carousels-beat-reels-for-engagement-in-2026-and-when-to-use-each/).

### (G) AI-visual explainers (the Zack D style clone pipeline)
- 0-1 s: a photoreal AI still or 5 s clip of the object mid-action, exactly mimicking (A). Tutorials break the process into "script, 4 to 6 scenes, one photoreal 3D-render prompt per scene, image-to-video, TTS narration, captions" (https://www.snaplama.com/blog/how-to-create-animation-videos-like-zack-d-films-in-2026, https://www.teknoding.com/2025/11/how-created-zack-d-filmsstyle-videos.html).
- 1-15 s: same beats as (A) but with fewer camera moves (image-to-video clips are 5 to 8 s and one move each).
- Sound: TTS narration; YouTube treats AI voiceover as permitted production assistance so long as there is human editorial input, and demonetises the fully automated version (https://typecast.ai/learn/youtube-ai-monetization-july-15-ypp-update/, https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-monetization-policy-2025).
- Weakness to avoid: character inconsistency between scenes and a generic voice; the clone tools sell "character consistency" as the feature (https://taletok.io/zack-d-films-ai-generator/).

### (H) Kids / education demos (TheDadLab, Steve Spangler, Science Sparks)
- 0-1 s: a child's hands and household objects, colour-saturated, top-down or eye-level table shot; no voice.
- 1-3 s: the surprising result (colour change, foam, float).
- 3-15 s: the setup shown in reverse or the steps, each 2 to 3 s.
- Ending: the result again, then a caption listing materials.
- Captions: step numbers; no narration (https://www.netinfluencer.com/sergei-urban-of-thedadlab/).
- Sound: upbeat royalty-free music.
- CTA: "save for the weekend".

### (I) Non-English leaders
The same archetypes apply; what differs is that (F) card accounts are proportionally larger in Spanish and Turkish (ense_de_ciencia 2M, Evrim Ağacı 796K), (C) dominates Brazil (Iamarino 1.7M, Sacani 2M, Loos 2.2M per https://www.usepora.com.br/melhores-influenciadores-de-ciencia), and Italy has a publisher-scale hybrid in Geopop, whose 3D Vesuvius eruption short reached nearly 2M views and whose 6 to 10 minute explainers are now its biggest hits (https://www.ilmattino.it/napoli/cronaca/la_futura_eruzione_vesuvio_video_simulazione_diventa_virale_web-5401531.html, https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/first-facebook-now-tiktok-how-geopop-built-7-million-strong-community-science-explainers). Instagram launched AI Hindi audio translation for Reels in 2025, lowering the cost of a Hindi track (https://www.identitykit.in/blog/how-to-grow-instagram-followers-india).

## 4. What a two-person, no-camera, no-lab operation can and cannot replicate

Assets available: AI image and video generation, code-driven animation (HTML canvas, three.js), ElevenLabs voice, no camera, no lab.

Cannot replicate (do not try to fake):
- Real experiments and real tissue (B, E). Their value is that the audience knows it is real; a generated version of a cadaver or a chemical reaction is the thing YouTube's 2025 policy and Instagram's AI labels penalise, and it will be called out in comments.
- On-camera personality (C). Talking-head accounts sell trust in a person; an AI presenter is exactly the "virtual influencer" category, which is a different product.
- Institutional exclusivity (D). NASA's footage is public domain and usable, but the account cannot be NASA. It can be the fastest, clearest explainer of what NASA released.

Can replicate, ranked by expected impact (highest first):
1. The (A)/(G) mechanism reveal. Zack D Films proves that a narrated 30 s cross-section of an everyday object or body process is the highest-ceiling science format on short video (23 shorts over 100M, https://zackdfilms.fandom.com/wiki/Zack_D._Films), and the clone tutorials show the pipeline is script, 4 to 6 photoreal scenes, image-to-video, TTS, captions (https://www.snaplama.com/blog/how-to-create-animation-videos-like-zack-d-films-in-2026). The differentiator must be editorial (a correct, sourced mechanism and a real "why") to stay on the right side of the inauthentic-content line (https://typecast.ai/learn/youtube-ai-monetization-july-15-ypp-update/).
2. Code-driven mechanism animation (three.js / canvas) as the "second hook". Nobody in the top tier does live, exact, parameterised physics on screen; Kurzgesagt draws it, Zack renders it. A real simulation (orbit, wave, diffusion, gear train) with numbers changing on screen is unique, cheap to iterate, and produces the big kinetic numbers that (C) creators flash by hand.
3. News-peg speed in (F)/(C) structure without a face. Abram's hook grammar ("X just happened. Here's why it matters") and Astro Alexandra's same-day reaction can be done with a voice and a generated or public-domain visual, since the hook is the claim, not the face (https://async.com/blog/why-cleo-abram-goes-viral/, https://www.favikon.com/blog/who-is-astro-alexandra). New Scientist proves an institution-less voice can hit 99M views in six months on Instagram with news-and-why-it-matters (https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/new-scientist-hits-milestone-25026).
4. Institution imagery re-cut (D). NASA, ESA, ESO and Webb images are free to use with credit; Ken Burns on a 4K still with a two-line caption is the cheapest high-retention asset in the set and was the whole basis of the (F) card accounts before they added text.
5. Karaoke captions and a fixed type system. This is a pure editing decision with a measured 15 to 30% completion lift (https://ascynd.io/en/blog/why-hormozi-captions-get-more-views).
6. Carousels for saves. Carousels lead reels on engagement and saves (https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagram-carousels-beat-reels-for-engagement-in-2026-and-when-to-use-each/); a generated hero image plus a mechanism diagram plus a source slide is fully replicable.
7. Sound design. A single signature bed and a consistent ElevenLabs voice give the recognisability Zack gets from his voice and track (https://taletok.io/zack-d-films-ai-generator/). ElevenLabs is production assistance, not a disqualifier, as long as scripts are human-edited (https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-monetization-policy-2025).
8. Debunk-the-viral format (E/C without the person). The 3I/ATLAS alien clips (5M+ views) created demand for a calm, sourced correction; a voice-plus-simulation account can own that lane in a way the tabloids cannot.

## 5. Style bible proposal for @hqrs_1

### 5.1 Hook frame rules (0 to 1 s)
- The subject is in frame at frame 1, already moving. No logo, no title card, no fade-in. Zack, physicsfun, NASA and IOHA all open on the object (section 3).
- One sentence of narration starts at 0.0 s and contains a verb of change ("just", "is about to", "breaks", "regained") in the Abram pattern (https://async.com/blog/why-cleo-abram-goes-viral/).
- One line of text, maximum 6 words, appears by 0.3 s and is a claim or a question, never a label. The "have you ever wondered" phrasing is parodied and should be avoided (https://celebtechbuzz.com/zack-d-films/).
- Motion by 0.5 s: push-in on a generated still, or the first frame of a generated clip already mid-action.
- Test: thumbnail readable at 25% zoom (carousel rule from https://carouselli.com/blog/instagram-carousel-best-practices, applied to the reel cover).

### 5.2 Visual layers (stack, back to front)
1. Base plate: generated photoreal still (3:4 or 9:16, 2160 px tall) with a 4 to 6 s Ken Burns move (push-in 8 to 12%, or a slow lateral drift), matching the (D) institution pace of 3 to 5 s per image.
2. Motion plate: generated 6 to 10 s clips for the subject mid-action (one camera move per clip, no dialogue, no faces), used for the hook and the payoff.
3. Mechanism plate: three.js / canvas animation of the actual process, rendered 60 fps, on a dark field, with a single accent colour, used as the second hook at 8 to 15 s. This layer is the signature; nobody in the top tier uses real simulations.
4. Number plate: kinetic figures (count-ups, unit flips, scale bars) in the display face, one number at a time, never a table.
5. Caption plate: karaoke captions (see 5.3).
6. Source plate: a one-line source in small type at the end card and in the caption text.

### 5.3 Caption style
- Word-by-word karaoke captions for narrated segments; one highlighted word at a time; 3 to 5 words per line; centred in the lower third but above the Instagram UI safe zone (https://www.opus.pro/blog/instagram-reels-caption-subtitle-best-practices).
- Static headline text (full line) only in the first second and on the end card.
- Weight: heavy sans, white with a 2 px dark outline or a translucent box; accent colour for the highlighted word and for numbers.
- Never more than two text elements on screen at once.

### 5.4 Colour and type
- Dark field (near-black blue) as the base, so generated images and simulations read as one system; one warm accent (amber) for numbers and highlights and one cool accent (cyan) for simulation lines. This is the Kurzgesagt logic of saturated accents on dark, without copying its palette (https://midlibrary.io/styles/kurzgesagt).
- Two typefaces only: a heavy geometric sans for headlines and numbers, a humanist sans for captions and sources. Fix sizes: headline 96 px, captions 64 px, sources 36 px on a 1080 x 1920 canvas.
- Generated stills are colour-graded to the same lift and contrast so the feed grid reads as one account (natgeo's grid consistency is the model).

### 5.5 Sound design
- One ElevenLabs voice, chosen once, used everywhere; pace 150 to 165 words per minute for explainers, faster (170 to 180) for news pegs; scripts of 65 to 95 words for 30 s (https://github.com/Anil-matcha/zack-d-films-ai-video-generator/blob/main/SKILL.md).
- One signature music bed (own composition or a licensed loop), at -18 LUFS under voice, recognisable within 2 s; a second bed for news posts.
- SFX: a single low "thud" on each mechanism reveal, a soft tick on each number change; no whooshes on every cut.
- Trending audio only on carousels and (F) card reels; never over the voice.

### 5.6 Pacing table (cuts per 10 s)
| Format | Length | Cuts per 10 s | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism reveal (A/G) | 25 to 40 s | 3 to 4 | camera move inside shots substitutes for cuts (Zack pattern) |
| News peg (C-without-face / F) | 15 to 30 s | 4 to 6 | claim, visual, why-it-matters, source |
| Image drop (D re-cut) | 8 to 15 s | 2 to 3 | 74% completion band (https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-instagram-reels-length) |
| Simulation short | 12 to 20 s | 1 to 2 | one continuous sim, numbers ticking |
| Debunk | 30 to 45 s | 4 to 5 | viral clip inset for 3 s max, then the real data |

Guardrails: first cut by 3 s; no shot over 5 s without internal motion (https://cutscore.io/blog/how-fast-should-i-cut-my-video).

### 5.7 Length
- Default 20 to 35 s (TikTok sweet spot 24 to 38 s, https://nealschaffer.com/tiktok-video-length/).
- Image drops 8 to 15 s for completion rate.
- One longer piece (60 to 90 s) per week for the mechanism series, since Geopop's data shows longer explainers can be the biggest hits once the audience trusts the account (https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/first-facebook-now-tiktok-how-geopop-built-7-million-strong-community-science-explainers).

### 5.8 End card
- Last 1.5 s: the payoff frame held, one line "Source: ..." in small type, the handle at lower left, no "follow" animation. The loop is the CTA, as in (A).
- News pegs end with the single line "Why it matters:" already answered; never end on a question the video did not answer.

### 5.9 Carousel cover rules
- Cover = generated hero image cropped so the mechanism is cut off at the edge, plus a 5-word claim; must "feel incomplete" (https://www.adpicto.com/en/blog/instagram-carousel-best-practices-2026).
- Slide 2 is the mechanism diagram (export of the three.js frame), slide 3 the number, last slide the source list and "save this".
- 4 to 7 slides; carousels are for saves and DM shares, reels for reach (https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagram-carousels-beat-reels-for-engagement-in-2026-and-when-to-use-each/).

### 5.10 Five signature devices (recognisable within three posts)
1. The live simulation window: every explainer contains a 4 to 8 s real physics simulation on the dark field with the amber numbers ticking. No top account does this.
2. The cross-section wipe: a vertical wipe from the photoreal still to the mechanism layer at the second hook, always the same direction and speed.
3. One big number: every post has exactly one kinetic number, counted up, in the display face, with its unit flipping once (metres to football pitches, years to human lifetimes).
4. The source stamp: a small fixed-position source line on the end card and in the first line of the caption. It is the anti-3I/ATLAS device and the trust signal a faceless account otherwise lacks.
5. The voice and the bed: one voice, one 2 s musical motif at 0 s and at the payoff. Zack's recognisability rests on voice plus track (https://taletok.io/zack-d-films-ai-generator/); this is the cheapest identity asset available to a two-person team.
