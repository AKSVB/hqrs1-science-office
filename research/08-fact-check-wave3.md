| 12 | OpenAI Navier-Stokes claim vs Fields Medalists' declaration (reel-ai-navier-stokes) | AMBER | **The claim.** Announced 8 Sept 2026 (OpenAI page "On the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem"; Nature news d41586-026-02842-5, 8 Sept; Quanta 8 Sept; CNBC 9 Sept). What was proved, in OpenAI's own words as reported: "an initially smooth fluid at rest can develop a singularity in a finite time" for the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations *under a smooth external force*: finite-time blowup with bounded kinetic energy (Tufts Daily; Nature; TheNextWeb; kingy.ai). It is a *negative* answer for the forced problem, i.e. the equations can break down. Which official cases: OpenAI says the construction establishes alternatives **C and D** of the Clay problem statement (forced blowup on R^3 and, by compact support, on the torus T^3); it does *not* claim the unforced alternatives A and B, which most mathematicians consider the deeper question (TheNextWeb; DataCamp; forkast; Medium/Oo). Paper: 166 pages plus a Lean 4 formalisation (Tufts; remio.ai; Medium/Oo). **Numbers, all OpenAI's own account:** "about 10,000" concurrent agents (Nature; Tufts; VentureBeat; CNBC); result reached after **about 88 hours** from launch, on Sat 5 Sept, agents launched about 1 Sept (CNBC; Tufts; The Neuron; ScienceTimes); **17 more hours** for Lean formalisation and verification (CNBC; Medium/McGuire; The Neuron). Nature also reports a first run of ~1,000 agents for ~50 hours on a simplified (unforced Euler) problem; not for screen. Other reported figures (2.7 million messages, ~130 billion tokens, "$22 million") are single-source or derived; do NOT use. **Prize:** OpenAI: "We do not intend to claim the Millennium Prize for this result" (OpenAI page as quoted by Medium/McGuire; Axios 8 Sept; TheNextWeb). The Clay Institute has not certified anything; its rules need publication, two years and community acceptance (The Neuron; Wikipedia). A claim that Clay "opened its verification process" on 11 Sept is single-source (Substack), unverified; omit. **Declaration:** "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics", posted 11 Sept 2026 on Terence Tao's blog and at mathandai.org, **25 initial signatories, all Fields Medallists** (Tao blog/Mathstodon; SciAm "25 winners of math's 'Nobel Prize'"; implicator.ai; Leiter Reports). One SciAm article says 26; use 25 with "initially" or "at launch". Exact wording: "The goals of the AI companies and the goals of the mathematical community are severely misaligned" and "the push by AI companies to solve mathematical problems as a benchmark is detrimental to the science of mathematics, and to the mathematical community" (implicator.ai; Lobsters/X quoting the text; Wikipedia priority-controversy page). It does NOT say "AI-benchmark mathematics is a severe misalignment"; that is our paraphrase and must not be shown in quotation marks. Several outlets frame it as a response to the OpenAI announcement; Tao says it grew out of discussions over the preceding week. Note Timothy Gowers publicly declined to sign (guest post on Tao's blog, 17 Sept), so "the maths community" is not unanimous. **Dispute:** Tristan Buckmaster (NYU) and Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) had an unpublished finite-time-blowup proof for the *forced Euler* equations (15 Aug); Buckmaster alleges OpenAI learned of it, that OpenAI's Sebastien Bubeck asked him to drop Alpöge as co-author, and that his Codex usage could have informed OpenAI; OpenAI denies using private data and says its work began 1 Sept and offered concurrent release on 6 Sept (TechCrunch 8 Sept; Axios; SciAm; VentureBeat; the-decoder; Science/AAAS). Call it a "credit and provenance dispute" or "priority dispute"; both are used. **Peer review:** not peer-reviewed; Lean-verified (which checks the formal statement follows from stated assumptions, not that the theorem matches the Clay problem); mathematicians still checking (Tufts Daily; remio.ai; SciAm). **May appear on screen:** "about 10,000 agents", "about 88 hours", "17 hours to formalise in Lean" (all labelled as OpenAI's own numbers), "8 Sept", "11 Sept", "25 Fields Medallists", "$1M", "Not peer-reviewed", "Cases C and D, forced; not A and B". | 13 | "Project Anchor" gravity hoax and the 12 Aug 2026 eclipse (carousel-project-anchor-debunk) | GREEN (framing AMBER) | **The hoax as it spread:** a "secret NASA document" called "Project Anchor", supposedly "leaked in November 2024", with a "$89 billion" budget, to survive a "7-second gravitational anomaly" on 12 Aug 2026 at "14:33 UTC" (10:33 a.m. EDT), caused by "intersecting gravitational waves from colliding black holes", with "40 million deaths from falls" (Snopes; Yahoo/Snopes syndication; Dexerto; Newsweek; Fact Crescendo). An early Instagram version said "$89 million" (Geo.tv); the billion figure is the one that spread. It circulated sporadically in late 2025 and went viral in early January 2026 on TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, X, Reddit, Threads, Bluesky and 4chan (Snopes; Newsweek; factually.co timeline). So it is a hoax *about* 12 Aug, not a hoax *of* 12 Aug; it was debunked in January and again around the date (Dexerto, "today"; Yahoo "this week"; Geo.tv, date unverified). **Debunks:** a NASA spokesperson by email to Snopes: "The Earth will not lose gravity on Aug. 12, 2026. Earth's gravity, or total gravitational force, is determined by its mass"; the only way to lose gravity would be for the Earth system (core, mantle, crust, ocean, terrestrial water, atmosphere) to lose mass (Snopes; Newsweek; BGR; NewsNation; Gizmodo). NASA senior science communicator Karen Fox to Newsweek: the theory "comes around every year" (Newsweek; Fact Crescendo). Snopes rating: False. A "local astronomy official" in West Texas debunked it on NewsWest9 (KWES, Midland-Odessa); the official's name and institution did NOT surface in any snippet and the page is proxy-blocked: **unverified**. Do not say "observatory officials"; say "NASA and a local astronomy official" or "NASA and fact-checkers". **The document:** no document, budget line or leak has ever been produced; Snopes found no reputable result for a "Project Anchor" leak in or after Nov 2024 and no 2024-25 social posts discussing one; Fact Crescendo reports searches of NASA press releases, technical reports and NTRS found nothing (Snopes; Fact Crescendo; Newsweek). The big number "0" is supportable as "0 NASA documents called Project Anchor have ever been found or produced"; it cannot be shown as a bare "0 exist" (a negative cannot be proved), so the sub-line must carry "found" or "produced". **The real event:** total solar eclipse of 12 Aug 2026; path of totality from a small region of northern Russia across the Arctic, eastern Greenland, western Iceland (incl. Reykjavik), the North Atlantic, the northern half of Spain and the northeast corner of Portugal (NASA science page; Wikipedia; nationaleclipse; National Geographic). Totality on the ground from about 16:58 to 18:34 UTC; greatest eclipse 17:46-17:47 UTC; maximum totality 2 min 18 s; Spain from about 18:27 UTC, shortly before sunset (NASA; Wikipedia; IGN Spain). First totality on mainland Europe since 1999 (NatGeo; nationaleclipse). So the hoax's 14:33 UTC is more than two hours *before* totality began anywhere. **Physics wording:** "Gravity is a property of mass. It cannot be switched off" is consistent with NASA's statement and with Snopes/IFLScience ("Earth's gravity cannot suddenly disappear without the planet itself losing its mass"; gravitational waves from black-hole mergers are far too weak to affect it).
